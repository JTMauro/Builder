Pattern check

Run the letter test twice: once from 3 m or more (far) and once at about 40 cm (near), with your usual glasses off or on, the same each time.

This is a screening aid, not a medical diagnosis. Book a proper eye exam with an optometrist.

Arm's-length test (near point and age)

Age-related loss of near focus (presbyopia) shows up as a near point that moves away. Do this with one eye covered and no reading glasses. Complete the calibration card above first.

Hold the screen at arm's length so the small text below is sharp. Bring it slowly closer until it first turns blurry. Stop and measure from your eye to the screen with a ruler or tape.

Nearsighted or farsighted? (red-green test)

Cover one eye, take your glasses off, and sit at the distance in the setup card (3 m or more works best; use the arrow keys and Enter). Look at the letters on the red and green halves. Which half is clearer?

Light of different colors focuses at slightly different depths, which is what this test uses. Farsighted eyes can refocus to cheat it, so treat it as a hint. Confirm with the distance and near results on tab 2.

Astigmatism

The line fan from tab 2: lines in one direction look darker or sharper.

Glaucoma screen (side vision)

Glaucoma usually dims side vision first, and you rarely notice. Cover one eye. Keep looking at the center dot. Press Space (or the button) each time a faint dot flashes anywhere. It takes about 1 minute per eye. Use the setup card distance, and sit about 30 to 40 cm from the screen so the sides fit.

Screen brightness and room light change what you can see, so this is only a rough screen. Missing a few dots can also be tiredness or looking away. It cannot rule glaucoma in or out; only an eye pressure check and optic nerve exam can.
